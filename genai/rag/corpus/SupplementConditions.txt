[CHUNK ID]   hypodontia-what-it-is
[TOPIC]      Hypodontia — being born without some teeth
[CONDITIONS] hypodontia
[KEYWORDS]   hypodontia, missing teeth, born without teeth, congenitally missing, gap, genetic, adult teeth, milk teeth
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Tried counting how many teeth your children have? They might naturally be missing a few" (26 Jun 2024) | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/news/patient-care/tried-counting-how-many-teeth-your-children-have-they-might-naturally-be-missing-a-few
[TRIGGERS]
  - What is hypodontia?
  - Why am I missing teeth?
  - Is it normal to be born without some teeth?

CONTENT:
Hypodontia means a person was born without one or more teeth. It usually affects
the adult (permanent) teeth, but it can also affect milk teeth. Wisdom teeth are not
counted. It is not rare: about 11 in every 100 patients who need braces at the
National Dental Centre Singapore have it. The teeth most often missing are the
small upper front teeth beside the two middle ones, then the lower second premolars,
then the lower front teeth.

The main cause is genetic, and it can run in families. It is also linked with some
syndromes. Less often, the tissue that forms a tooth is damaged early in life, for
example by injury, infection, radiation or chemotherapy.

A photo cannot tell a tooth that never formed from one that was lost or has not come
through yet. A dentist confirms hypodontia with an X-ray.

--- CHUNK S02 ---
[CHUNK ID]   hypodontia-effects-and-when-to-see
[TOPIC]      Missing teeth — effects, and when to see a dentist
[CONDITIONS] hypodontia
[KEYWORDS]   missing teeth, gap, tooth drift, chewing, speech, food trapping, late teeth, X-ray, children, age 6 to 12
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Tried counting how many teeth your children have? They might naturally be missing a few" (26 Jun 2024) | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/news/patient-care/tried-counting-how-many-teeth-your-children-have-they-might-naturally-be-missing-a-few
[TRIGGERS]
  - Is it bad to have a gap from a missing tooth?
  - Do I need to replace a missing tooth?
  - When should my child see a dentist about missing teeth?

CONTENT:
Missing teeth can make chewing and speaking harder, let food get trapped, and allow
the teeth next to the gap to drift. A gap does not always need to be filled,
especially in children who still have their milk teeth.

See a dentist if baby or adult teeth are very late to appear, if there are gaps, or
if an adult tooth that should have come through has not. For children, gaps or delays
between about age 6 and 12 are a good reason for a dental visit. Dentists often take
a full-mouth X-ray at around age eight to check which adult teeth are forming.

Treatment depends on age. Braces are usually avoided in very young children, and
implants are normally left until the jaw has finished growing. Adults may be offered a
denture or a bridge. The dentist talks through the options; leaving the gap is
sometimes one of them.

--- CHUNK S03 ---
[CHUNK ID]   missing-teeth-replacement-options
[TOPIC]      Replacing missing teeth — implant, bridge or denture
[CONDITIONS] hypodontia
[KEYWORDS]   tooth replacement, implant, bridge, resin-bonded bridge, denture, partial denture, complete denture, missing tooth
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Missing Teeth and Tooth Replacement" | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/missing-teeth-tooth-replacement-dental-implants-bridges-dentures
[TRIGGERS]
  - How can a missing tooth be replaced?
  - What is the difference between an implant, a bridge and a denture?
  - How long does it take to replace a missing tooth?

CONTENT:
There are three main ways to replace missing teeth. Replacing them helps with
eating, speech and appearance.

Implant: a small titanium post is placed in the jaw bone and a new tooth is fixed on
top. It usually takes about four months to a year, and some people need a bone graft
first.

Bridge: a fixed false tooth held by the teeth on either side, which are usually
shaped to take crowns. A resin-bonded bridge needs much less trimming of the
neighbouring teeth. A bridge needs at least two visits. Keep it clean by brushing,
flossing and having regular check-ups.

Denture: a removable plate. A partial denture replaces some teeth and a complete
denture replaces all the teeth in one jaw. It usually takes two to six visits.

Before choosing, the dentist checks the jaw bone, how the teeth and jaws meet, habits
such as grinding, and general health.

[CHUNK ID]   mouth-ulcer-what-it-is
[TOPIC]      Mouth ulcers — what they are and common causes
[CONDITIONS] mouth_ulcer
[KEYWORDS]   mouth ulcer, ulcer, sore, canker sore, cold sore, bite cheek, recurring ulcers, stress, causes
[SOURCE]     SGH (Sengkang General Hospital Head and Neck Service): "Mouth Ulcers – Causes & When to See A Doctor" | https://www.sgh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/mouth-ulcers
[TRIGGERS]
  - What is a mouth ulcer?
  - What causes mouth ulcers?
  - Why do I keep getting mouth ulcers?

CONTENT:
A mouth ulcer is a sore where part of the soft lining of the mouth has broken down.
A single ulcer is most often caused by a small injury, such as accidentally biting
the cheek, and it usually heals by itself within one to two weeks.

Some people get ulcers again and again. Often no clear cause is found, but some
health conditions make repeat ulcers more likely, including viral infections such as
cold sores, low levels of some nutrients, some medicines, bowel inflammation and a
few rarer conditions. Stress and periods can bring on cold sores in people who carry
the virus.

--- CHUNK S05 ---
[CHUNK ID]   mouth-ulcer-when-to-see
[TOPIC]      Mouth ulcers — when to see a doctor or dentist
[CONDITIONS] mouth_ulcer
[KEYWORDS]   ulcer not healing, two weeks, three weeks, oral cancer, smoking, alcohol, betel nut, see a doctor, raised edges
[SOURCE]     SGH (Sengkang General Hospital Head and Neck Service): "Mouth Ulcers – Causes & When to See A Doctor" | https://www.sgh.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/mouth-ulcers
[TRIGGERS]
  - When should I worry about a mouth ulcer?
  - How long does a mouth ulcer take to heal?
  - Can a mouth ulcer be cancer?

CONTENT:
Most mouth ulcers heal on their own within one to two weeks. See a doctor or dentist
if an ulcer has not healed after two to three weeks. A sore that will not heal needs
to be checked because, occasionally, it can be a sign of mouth cancer. An ulcer with
raised or rolled edges should also be checked.

Smoking, drinking alcohol and chewing betel nut raise the risk of mouth and tongue
cancer, but a non-healing ulcer should be checked even if you have none of these
habits. Only a doctor or dentist can say what an ulcer is; a photo cannot.

--- CHUNK S06 ---
[CHUNK ID]   discoloured-teeth-causes
[TOPIC]      Discoloured teeth — surface stains vs stains inside the tooth
[CONDITIONS] tooth_discoloration
[KEYWORDS]   discoloured teeth, discolouration, stains, yellow teeth, brown teeth, grey tooth, extrinsic, intrinsic, coffee, tea, smoking, ageing, trauma
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Discoloured Teeth" | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/discoloured-teeth
[TRIGGERS]
  - Why are my teeth stained?
  - What causes yellow or brown teeth?
  - Why has one tooth turned grey?

CONTENT:
Tooth stains are either on the surface of the tooth (extrinsic) or inside it
(intrinsic).

Surface stains come from strongly coloured food and drink such as coffee, tea, red
wine, cola and curry, from smoking or other tobacco use, from stain-forming bacteria,
and from not cleaning the teeth well. They usually cause no symptoms apart from how
the teeth look.

Stains inside the tooth can come from ageing, genes, an injury to the tooth, tooth
decay, problems while the tooth was forming in childhood (such as too much fluoride,
or high fevers), or a tooth that has had root canal treatment slowly changing colour.
A single tooth that turns darker after a knock is a stain inside the tooth, so a
dentist should check it.
If discolouration comes with toothache, sensitivity, or a hole or pit in the tooth,
it may be decay, so get it checked rather than treating it as a cosmetic issue.

[CHUNK ID]   discoloured-teeth-what-helps
[TOPIC]      Discoloured teeth — what helps
[CONDITIONS] tooth_discoloration
[KEYWORDS]   remove stains, whitening, bleaching, scaling and polishing, veneers, crowns, stop smoking, cut down coffee
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Discoloured Teeth" | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/symptoms-treatments/discoloured-teeth
[TRIGGERS]
  - How can I get rid of tooth stains?
  - Can a cleaning remove stains?
  - Does teeth whitening work on all stains?

CONTENT:
At home: stop smoking, cut down on coffee, tea and other strongly coloured food and
drink, and brush well every day.

At the dentist: a scaling and polishing visit removes most surface stains. Stains
inside the tooth cannot be polished away. Some of them can be lightened by bleaching
(whitening). If bleaching does not work, a dentist may suggest a veneer or a crown.
The dentist decides which option suits your teeth.

[CHUNK ID]   dental-emergency-where-to-go
[TOPIC]      Dental emergencies — when to go to A&E and where
[CONDITIONS] all
[KEYWORDS]   emergency, A&E, 995, facial swelling, fever, uncontrolled bleeding, knocked out tooth, broken tooth, trauma, severe pain, NDCS, SGH emergency, after hours, KKH
[SOURCE]     NDCS: "Emergency Dental Services" | https://www.ndcs.com.sg/clinic-visit/emergency-dental-services
[TRIGGERS]
  - What counts as a dental emergency?
  - Where can I get emergency dental care at night?
  - Should I go to A&E for a toothache?

CONTENT:
Get emergency care straight away for: swelling of the face or jaw from an infection,
especially with a high fever; bleeding from the mouth that will not stop; severe
pain; or an injury to the teeth or face, such as a loose, broken or knocked-out tooth
or a cut to the gums or face. If breathing or swallowing is difficult, call 995.

The National Dental Centre Singapore (NDCS) has 24-hour emergency dental care on the
SingHealth Outram campus. During NDCS clinic hours, its Urgent Care Clinic sees
toothaches, injuries and infections from teeth. Outside those hours, urgent dental
cases go to the SGH Emergency Department. Children under 12 go to NDCS Paediatric
Dentistry during clinic hours and to KK Women's and Children's Hospital Children's
Emergency after hours. Emergency departments do not do routine work such as fillings
or root canals.

A toothache on its own is usually best seen at a dental clinic, unless it comes with
a high fever or a lot of facial swelling.

[CHUNK ID]   toothache-while-waiting
[TOPIC]      Tooth or gum pain — what to do while waiting to see a dentist
[CONDITIONS] caries, all
[KEYWORDS]   toothache, tooth pain, gum pain, salt water rinse, cold pack, swelling, walk-in, GP, polyclinic, pus, foul taste
[SOURCE]     SingHealth GPFirst: "Gum or Tooth Pain" | https://www.singhealth.com.sg/community-care/find-resources-in-your-community/what-is-gpfirst/conditions-your-gp-can-treat/gum-or-tooth-pain
[TRIGGERS]
  - What can I do for a toothache at home?
  - My gum hurts, what should I do?
  - Should I see a GP or a dentist for tooth pain?

CONTENT:
Most tooth and gum pain gets better with early care, and many dental clinics offer
walk-in or next-day appointments, so book a dentist soon.

While you wait: rinse with warm salt water (about half a teaspoon of salt in a glass
of warm water). If the cheek or jaw is swollen, hold a cold pack wrapped in a towel
against the outside of the cheek for 15 to 20 minutes. Avoid very hot or very cold
food and drinks, and hard, sugary or acidic food. Keep brushing twice a day and
cleaning between your teeth.

See a GP or polyclinic doctor promptly if the pain comes with clear swelling of the
jaw, fever, pus or a bad taste, or if a tooth broke in an accident. Go to the
Emergency Department if the gums or a tooth socket keep bleeding even after pressing
on them with gauze. For advice on pain relief, ask a pharmacist or doctor.
